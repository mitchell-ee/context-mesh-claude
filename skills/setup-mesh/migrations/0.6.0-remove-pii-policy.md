# 0.6.0 — remove the `PII policy` line from the Hub root index

**Applies when:** the Hub root `context-index.md` has a `**PII policy:**` line (either
`strip` or `enrich`).
**Guard:** a root index with no such line is already migrated — skip it. If the Hub root has
no `PII policy:` line, this migration is a no-op: say so and move on.
**Requires:** —
**Precedes:** —

## What changed

Vocabulary v2.4 removed the `strip | enrich` PII policy and, with it, stage 1b of ingestion.
**Ingestion now does nothing to a transcript before extracting from it** — no redaction, no
anonymization, no substitution of speaker names.

The setting arrived on 2026-06-25 as an unexamined GDPR default and was never justified
against the actual material: transcripts come from meetings whose participants are internal or
have consented to recording. Worse, `strip` was the *default*, so the safe-looking option was
also the lossy one — it replaced "Sarah", "Sarah K." and "SK" with a label rather than
resolving them to one person.

That reframing is the real change: **normalizing a transcript is a quality concern, not a
privacy posture.** The useful form of it belongs in a pre-pass, not buried in ingestion.

## What this migration does

**It removes one line from one file.** Nothing else.

Specifically: delete the `- **PII policy:** strip` (or `enrich`) line from the **Hub root**
`context-index.md`'s Identity block, and the explanatory HTML comment beside it if present.

In particular:

- **Domain indexes are not touched.** The policy was root-only; a domain index never carried
  it. If one somehow does, report it rather than editing — that is a hand-authored line whose
  intent this migration cannot know.
- **No transcript, archive, or candidate is touched.** A mesh that ingested under `strip` has
  archived transcripts with redacted speaker names. **Those stay exactly as they are.**
  Re-redacting is impossible and un-redacting is fiction: the original names are gone.
  Content already written under the old policy is history, and history is not migrated.

## The one thing to tell the human

**Behaviour changes on the next ingestion run, and it changes in the direction of keeping
more.** Under `strip`, participant names were redacted before anything was written. They no
longer are, and an `archived` transcript is now stored **as received**.

Say that plainly. Someone whose mesh has been running `strip` chose it, even if only by
accepting the default, and should hear that the default has moved rather than discover it in a
committed transcript.

Then say what the lever is now: **`.gitignore`.** Retention is a decision about a repo the
team owns, not a setting in the pipeline.

## Procedure

1. **Read the Hub root `context-index.md`** and look for a `**PII policy:**` line.
2. **If absent** — no-op. Say so and stop.
3. **If present**, show the human the line, and say which policy the mesh had been running.
4. **On approval**, delete the line and the adjacent explanatory comment.
5. **Report the behaviour change** described above, including the `.gitignore` lever.
6. **Report, without editing:** any *domain* index carrying a `PII policy:` line, and whether
   `staging/candidates/` holds archived transcripts written under the old policy (they are
   fine, they are simply older — this is context, not a defect).

## The trap to avoid

**Match the field, never the phrase.** `- **PII policy:** strip` is a declaration; the words
"PII" or "strip" in a sentence are prose. A context file that discusses data handling, or an
index whose *Not in this mesh* section names `governance/data-handling.md`, must be left
completely alone. Anchor on the `- **PII policy:**` line shape at the start of a line.

## Idempotence

Guarded on the presence of a `PII policy:` line in the Hub root index. Once removed, a re-run
finds nothing and does nothing. Because the migration touches no transcript and no candidate,
running it twice cannot compound — there is exactly one line, and it is either there or it is
not.
